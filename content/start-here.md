You do not need to fix every part of your finances this weekend. Pick the decision in front of you and take one useful step.

## 1. Get a handle on the month ahead

Start with your actual take-home income, then account for rent, food, transport, bills, and a little breathing room. If your income changes with the semester, plan around a conservative month.

- [Build a student budget](/tools/student-budget/)
- [Budget on a tight income](/posts/how-to-budget-in-college-on-a-tight-budget/)
- [Build an emergency fund](/posts/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund-in-your-20s/)

## 2. Understand credit before applying

A first card should help you build a payment history without encouraging spending you cannot repay. Compare eligibility, fees, and repayment habits before rewards.

- [Student credit cards with no credit history](/posts/best-credit-card-for-college-students-with-no-credit-history/)
- [Build credit from scratch](/posts/how-to-build-credit-in-college-from-scratch/)
- [Student cards vs. secured cards](/posts/secured-credit-card-vs-student-credit-card/)

## 3. Plan for internship income

Temporary income needs to cover temporary costs and the gap after the job ends. Start with housing and transport, then decide what is available to save.

- [What to do with your first internship paycheck](/posts/what-to-do-with-first-paycheck-from-internship/)
- [Download the internship budget worksheet](/tools/internship-budget/)

## 4. Get ready for life after graduation

Know when benefits start, when loan payments are due, and how much cash your next move requires. Invest after considering expensive debt and near-term cash needs.

- [Your graduation money checklist](/posts/what-to-do-financially-before-graduating-college/)
- [Understand a Roth IRA](/posts/how-to-open-a-roth-ira-as-a-college-student/)
- [Understand your student loans](/posts/how-to-understand-your-student-loans/)
