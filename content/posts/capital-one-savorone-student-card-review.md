Capital One currently markets Savor Rewards for Students. This page keeps its original URL so existing links continue to work; the older SavorOne wording is not used as proof of a current offer.

## Who should look at this option?

A category-based card may be useful if most of your existing spending falls in eligible categories. Compare it with a flat-rate student card on the provider's current comparison page. Rewards on spending you would not otherwise make are not savings.

## Check the details that affect your result

Read which stores count as groceries, whether particular transactions are excluded, and what rate applies elsewhere. Capital One's student comparison explains its grocery-store exclusions. A store selling food is not automatically an eligible grocery merchant.

Read the annual fee, APR, foreign transaction terms, and bonus conditions on the offer you would actually use. Different application paths can display different offers. This guide does not promise approval or recommend applying solely for a bonus.

## Compare your own spending

Write down last month's food, entertainment, and other spending. Apply each offer's published rates to eligible purchases, and compare the result after fees. Keep the same spending assumptions for both cards.

If that exercise feels cumbersome, a flat-rate option may be easier to manage. If you expect to carry a balance, focus first on borrowing costs and a repayment plan rather than rewards.
