Start with records rather than a repayment strategy. You need to know who holds each loan, what type it is, and what your agreement actually requires.

## Build a loan inventory

For every loan, record the lender or servicer, loan type, balance, interest rate, required payment, and next due date. Identify whether the rate is fixed or variable. Use your official account statements and Federal Student Aid account for federal records.

Federal and private loans are not interchangeable. A private lender's marketing page does not define the repayment options on a federal loan.

## Separate payment timing from interest

Not owing a payment while in school does not necessarily mean interest stops. Federal subsidized and unsubsidized loans treat in-school interest differently; private terms depend on the agreement.

Check how interest accrues and the circumstances when unpaid interest can be added to principal. Do not assume that every loan capitalizes interest at graduation in the same way.

## Review current options before a change

For federal repayment questions, use current Federal Student Aid resources and your servicer. Program availability and eligibility can change; an old plan comparison is not an enrollment guarantee.

If you consider private refinancing, distinguish it from federal consolidation. Refinancing federal debt into a private loan can remove federal protections. A lower monthly payment may also reflect a longer repayment term rather than lower total cost.

## Make the payment fit the budget

Put required payments and actual due dates into your budget. Contact the servicer early if you cannot pay; do not assume an application for assistance has already changed your obligation.
