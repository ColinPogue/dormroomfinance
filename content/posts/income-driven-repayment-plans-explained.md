**Do not select a federal repayment plan from a static list in an old article.** Program rules, application availability, and legal developments can change. This guide explains how to gather the information needed for a current decision; it does not certify which plans you can enroll in today.

## Start with your own loan records

Identify each federal loan type, servicer, balance, current repayment status, and required payment. Private loans are governed by their own agreements and do not become eligible for federal income-driven repayment just because the payment is difficult to afford.

Use the official Federal Student Aid repayment pages and your authenticated account. If a tool shows an estimate, confirm the assumptions and whether it reflects the current status of the relevant program.

## Compare more than the monthly payment

Ask about eligibility, how income is assessed, required documentation, recertification, interest treatment, repayment duration, and any forgiveness conditions. A low monthly payment does not itself prove that the total cost is lower.

Do not assume a projected forgiven amount will be tax-free. Tax treatment depends on the program, timing, and applicable federal and state rules. Get current tax guidance before relying on a projection.

## Confirm the action and effective date

Keep a copy of any application and official response. An application does not by itself prove a payment obligation has changed. Ask the servicer what you must pay while a request is pending and get the answer through official channels.

If you receive conflicting information, document dates and messages and seek help from official federal resources or a qualified student-loan counselor.
