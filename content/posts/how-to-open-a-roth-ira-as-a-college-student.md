A Roth IRA is a type of retirement account, not an investment by itself. Opening one can be useful for eligible students, but the account must fit your cash needs and the tax rules.

## Check eligibility before contributing

IRA contributions are limited by applicable tax-year rules, including eligible compensation and annual limits. Roth contributions can also be restricted by income and filing status. Check the current IRS guidance instead of treating a limit copied from an older article as current.

A summer paycheck may provide eligible compensation, but gifts and transfers are not automatically compensation. If your situation is unusual, obtain qualified tax guidance before contributing.

## Keep near-term money available

Consider expensive debt, required bills, and a cash cushion before committing money to retirement. The right amount for someone with uncertain hours and a rent deposit due may differ from the amount for someone with fully covered expenses.

Do not choose an investment assuming you can sell it at a profit whenever you need the cash. Markets can fall at the wrong time.

## Compare an account, then choose investments

Check account fees, available investments, trading costs, minimums, and transfer procedures. A low account fee does not mean every investment inside it has no expenses.

After opening and funding an account, check whether the money remains in cash. Buying an investment is a separate decision. Consider diversification, costs, risk, and how long the money can remain invested.

## Keep the tax year straight

Track the year each contribution applies to and total contributions across your IRAs. Review IRS rules for contribution deadlines, excess contributions, and distributions. Do not assume every withdrawal from a Roth IRA is tax-free just because some withdrawals can qualify.
