This page is a decision checklist for someone considering Earnest or another private refinance lender. We have not verified a current personalized Earnest rate or tested an application, and this update does not rank Earnest against the full market.

## First identify what you would replace

Refinancing federal loans into a private loan removes federal benefits and protections. That tradeoff matters before comparing lenders. Federal consolidation is a separate process and should not be confused with a private refinance.

If the existing loans are private, read their current agreements too. Payment flexibility, co-signer terms, and other protections can differ between lenders.

## Ask for the actual offered terms

An advertised starting rate is not your offer. Review the fixed or variable rate, fees, repayment period, monthly payment, and total repayment amount in your own disclosures. Check whether a quote requires a hard credit inquiry.

A lower monthly payment can come from stretching the term. Compare equivalent repayment periods before concluding you would save overall.

## Check flexibility and affordability

Ask what happens if income falls, whether a co-signer is involved, and what the agreement permits. Do not rely on a promised refinance later if the initial terms are already difficult to afford.

If federal protections may be valuable to you, seek current official guidance before converting those loans to private debt. Read [the refinancing comparison checklist](/posts/how-to-decide-if-student-loan-refinancing-is-worth-it/) first.
