I'll be honest: I don't have student loans to refinance yet. I'm still in school. But I work in finance, I've spent this summer watching people make expensive decisions with incomplete information, and student loan refinancing is one of those areas where a bad call can cost you thousands. So I dug into Earnest specifically because it comes up constantly when people talk about refinancing, and I wanted to know if the hype is real.

Short answer: Earnest is genuinely one of the better options out there for refinancing, but it's not the right move for everyone. The decision to refinance federal loans especially deserves more thought than most people give it. If you haven't already read through [how to decide if student loan refinancing is worth it](/posts/how-to-decide-if-student-loan-refinancing-is-worth-it/), start there before you go any further.

## How Earnest Stacks Up Against the Competition

| Lender | Best For | Key Detail |

## What Earnest Actually Offers

Earnest was founded in 2013 and acquired by Navient in 2017, which some people find off-putting given Navient's reputation. Fair concern. But Earnest operates independently and the product itself has stayed strong. They refinance both federal and private student loans, and they'll work with loan balances as low as $5,000 up to $500,000 for people with advanced degrees.

The rate range as of late 2026 is 4.44% to 9.99% APR for fixed loans and 4.99% to 9.99% APR for variable. Where you land in that range depends on your credit score, income, debt-to-income ratio, and a few other factors. You need a minimum 650 credit score to qualify, though realistically you'll want to be closer to 700 or above to get rates that make refinancing worth it.

### The Precision Pricing Model

The thing that actually sets Earnest apart is what they call precision pricing. Most lenders offer you a handful of term options, say 5, 10, or 15 years, and you pick one. Earnest lets you dial in your exact monthly payment and they build the term around that number. So if you can afford $600 per month and that works out to an 8-year-and-3-month payoff, that's what they'll offer you.

This is genuinely useful if you're the kind of person who thinks about optimization at a detailed level. You're not forced to round up to a 10-year term and pay more interest than you need to. At least in my experience watching people manage debt, the ability to match a payment to your actual cash flow matters more than most people realize until they're in repayment.

### The Skip-a-Payment Feature

Earnest also lets you skip one payment per year without penalty, as long as you've made at least six consecutive on-time payments before using it. The interest still accrues, so you're not getting out of anything, you're just moving it. But if you have an irregular income month or an unexpected expense, it's a useful pressure valve. Not life-changing, but better than nothing.

### What You Give Up by Refinancing Federal Loans

This part matters a lot and I want to be direct about it. If you refinance federal loans with Earnest or anyone else, those loans become private. That means you lose access to income-driven repayment plans, Public Service Loan Forgiveness, and federal forbearance programs. These aren't hypothetical benefits. For a lot of people they're worth real money.

If you're going into a government job, nonprofit work, or any field where PSLF might apply, do not refinance your federal loans. Full stop. If you have solid income, a stable career path, and you're confident you don't need federal protections, the math on refinancing can work strongly in your favor. But know what you're trading away. The [breakdown of federal versus private student loans](/posts/federal-vs-private-student-loans-differences/) is worth reading if you're fuzzy on the specifics.

### The Application Process

Checking your rate with Earnest is a soft credit pull, so it doesn't affect your score. The full application triggers a hard pull, same as any lender. The process is online and reasonably straightforward. You'll need to upload proof of income, your most recent student loan statements, and standard identity documents. Approval usually takes a few business days, and you can expect about 30 days from application to your first payment going to Earnest instead of your old servicer.

One thing I'd actually do: check rates at two or three lenders simultaneously. SoFi, Splash Financial, and Earnest all do soft pulls for rate checks. Run them in parallel, compare the actual numbers you're offered, not the advertised ranges, and then decide. The advertised floor rates are for borrowers with excellent credit. Your number might be meaningfully different.

## Who Earnest Is and Isn't Right For

Earnest works best if you have solid credit (700 or above), stable income, federal loans you've already decided you don't need protection on, or private loans with higher rates you're trying to escape. The precision pricing model and no-fee structure make it genuinely competitive if you qualify for their better rates.

It's probably not your best option if you have federal loans and any realistic chance you'll want income-driven repayment or forgiveness. It's also not great if your credit is below 680 or your income is inconsistent, because you either won't qualify or you'll get a rate that isn't worth the trade-offs.

I'd also say: if you're fresh out of school and still figuring out your income trajectory, there's no rush. Refinancing is not a one-time-only opportunity. Wait until you have a year or two of stable income history and a clearer picture of your career direction. The rate environment might be better or worse by then, but your negotiating position almost certainly will be better. Pairing refinancing with an aggressive repayment strategy is where people really accelerate their progress, and [how to pay off student loans fast after graduation](/posts/how-to-pay-off-student-loans-fast-after-graduation/) goes into the mechanics of that if you're ready to think about it.

My first internship paycheck had $340 withheld in taxes I hadn't accounted for. I spent an hour that night actually reading about FICA and federal withholding, because spending plans built on gross numbers are how people get into trouble. The same logic applies here. Build your refinancing math on your real take-home number, your actual monthly payment, and the total interest you'll pay over the life of the loan. Not the headline rate. The total cost.

Earnest is a solid product. It's not magic. But if you qualify for their better rates and you've made a clear-eyed decision about federal loan trade-offs, it's worth a serious look.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does Earnest charge any fees for refinancing?**
Earnest charges no origination fees, no prepayment penalties, and no late fees. The rate you're quoted is the rate you pay.

**Q: What credit score do I need to refinance with Earnest?**
The minimum is 650, but to get rates that actually make refinancing worthwhile you'll want to be at 700 or above. Earnest also looks at income, employment, and your overall financial profile, not just the score.

**Q: Can I refinance both federal and private loans together with Earnest?**
Yes, Earnest will consolidate federal and private loans into a single new private loan. Just remember that refinancing federal loans means you permanently lose federal protections and repayment options.

**Q: How long does it take for Earnest to pay off my existing loans after I'm approved?**
Typically around 30 days from application to your first payment going to Earnest. During that window, keep making payments on your existing loans to avoid any late payment issues.

**Q: Is Earnest legit given that Navient owns it?**
Earnest operates independently and has maintained its own product and customer service structure since the acquisition. Navient's reputation is deservedly complicated, but Earnest's reviews and product quality have held up. I'd still rate-shop against SoFi and Splash Financial before committing.
