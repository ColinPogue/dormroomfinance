Building credit starts with understanding what is reported about you and taking on only payments you can manage. You do not need several cards or interest charges to make the process meaningful.

## Start with your credit report

A credit report records information about accounts and payment history. It is different from a credit score, which is calculated from information using a scoring model. Check for errors before assuming a low or missing score is just a lack of history.

Use the CFPB's credit-report resources to understand your records and official access options. Correct inaccurate information rather than opening more accounts to try to offset it.

## Choose a manageable route

A student or secured card may be one route if you meet the issuer's requirements. Compare current fees, eligibility, and deposit needs. A secured deposit ties up cash, so do not use emergency or rent money for it.

Being an authorized user is a different arrangement from being the primary borrower. Discuss spending permission, payment responsibility, and whether information is reported before relying on it as a strategy. Results are not guaranteed.

## Build a routine you can repeat

Keep purchases within money you have set aside. Review the statement, know the due date, and consider payment reminders or full-statement autopay when your cash flow supports it. Check that payments complete and keep balances manageable relative to your limit.

Do not pay interest just to demonstrate that you use a card. Do not assume a certain utilization percentage is a guarantee of a particular score. Your full file and the scoring model matter.

## Measure the right thing

Success is a sustainable record of responsible borrowing. A promised score in six months is not a reliable plan. Focus on accurate reports, affordable balances, and meeting obligations.
