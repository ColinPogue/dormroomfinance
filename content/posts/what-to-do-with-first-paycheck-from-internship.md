**Treat internship pay as temporary income with a job to do.** Before investing or upgrading your lifestyle, work out what it must cover until your next reliable paycheck.

## Start with the amount that reaches your account

Gross pay is not take-home pay. Check the pay period, deductions, and whether your first check covers a full period. Do not sign a housing commitment based solely on a quoted annual salary equivalent.

List immediate costs: rent, deposit, commuting, meals, and moving. A refundable deposit is still cash unavailable while it is held. Check when it is expected back rather than assuming it can fund your next move.

## Budget for the whole placement

Estimate total take-home income and total costs over the internship. Add a reserve for the weeks after it ends. The [internship budget worksheet](/tools/internship-budget/) helps separate recurring expenses, upfront costs, and that post-job reserve.

In a hypothetical ten-week placement at $700 take-home per week, total income is $7,000. Housing of $3,000, other living costs of $2,000, moving costs of $400, and a $1,000 reserve leave $600 before any deposit or additional expenses.

## Decide what is truly available to save or invest

First consider required payments and accessible cash for near-term needs. Money for next semester's rent has a different job from money you can leave invested for years.

If you are considering an IRA, check the IRS's compensation and contribution rules for the relevant tax year. Opening an account, funding it, and selecting investments are separate steps. A deposit left as cash is not automatically invested.

## Give the next paycheck a repeatable plan

Once you have a full pay period and actual spending to compare, automate only the transfers you can comfortably sustain. Revisit the plan if your hours, living costs, or internship end date change.
