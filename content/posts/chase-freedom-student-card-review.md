**Do not use an old Freedom Student review as a current application guide.** Chase's current student guidance says it does not offer a dedicated student credit card and identifies Freedom Rise as an option for people new to credit.

The old version of this article described Freedom Student rewards, bonuses, and an APR range as though new students could apply. That framing has been removed.

## If you are choosing your first card

Go to Chase's current product pages and read the offer, eligibility criteria, pricing, and fees. A familiar product name in an older comparison is not evidence that the offer remains available.

Compare the current option with other student or secured cards using [our first-card guide](/posts/best-credit-card-for-college-students-with-no-credit-history/). A provider relationship does not guarantee approval or a later upgrade.

## If you already have an account

Use the account agreement and notices attached to your own account. Existing-cardholder terms can differ from the terms of currently marketed products. Ask Chase about any conversion or upgrade, including fees and whether an application or credit inquiry is involved.

Do not close an account simply because an article recommends something newer. Consider its costs, how you use it, and the implications for your credit history before changing it.

## A practical comparison checklist

- Can you meet the current application requirements?
- Is there an annual fee, and what other fees apply?
- Can you pay the statement balance from money you already have?
- Are the rewards useful without increasing spending?
- Are any deposit, bonus, or upgrade conditions clearly understood?
