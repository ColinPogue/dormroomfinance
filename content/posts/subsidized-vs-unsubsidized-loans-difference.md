The central difference is responsibility for interest during eligible periods. Direct Subsidized Loans provide an interest benefit in qualifying circumstances, including eligible in-school enrollment. With Direct Unsubsidized Loans, borrowers are responsible for interest during all periods.

## Why the distinction matters

A pause before required monthly payments is not the same as an interest subsidy. Review your loan records so you understand which balances can grow while you are studying.

Do not assume a single current interest rate applies to every federal loan you hold. Disbursement date and loan type matter. Use the rate on the actual loan record and the official federal rate table.

## Compare the offer before accepting

Check financial-need requirements, annual and aggregate limits, fees, and the amount you actually need to borrow. An award is not a requirement to borrow the maximum.

If a term is unclear, ask your school's financial aid office to explain the specific award rather than relying on a generic example. Keep a copy of the explanation and official disclosures.

## Understand unpaid interest

Interest accrual and capitalization are distinct. Accrual is interest building up; capitalization adds unpaid interest to principal in applicable circumstances. The earlier version of this article generalized when capitalization happens. Check the rules for your loan and situation rather than assuming it occurs automatically at graduation.
