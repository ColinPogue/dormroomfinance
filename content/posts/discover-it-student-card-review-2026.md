A product review is only useful if the offer still matches what an applicant can get. Start at Discover's current card pages rather than treating an older review as an application offer.

## Student and secured are different descriptions

A student card is marketed toward students under the issuer's eligibility rules. A secured card involves a security deposit. Neither description guarantees approval, a particular limit, or a future credit score.

If you are evaluating the Discover secured product, use its own current disclosures. Do not copy a deposit minimum, reward rate, or upgrade timeline from a different Discover card or an older review.

## Read the offer in front of you

Check the annual fee, purchase APR, deposit requirement if applicable, rewards caps and activation conditions, and any matching or welcome offer. Confirm that the application page is for the exact product you intend to request.

This update does not reproduce a live APR or claim that one Discover product is the best option for all students. Compare the current product with alternatives on eligibility, cost, and repayment practicality.

## Make the repayment plan first

Consider a low, predictable category of spending that you already have cash to cover. Set reminders and inspect statements. Any reward becomes less useful if the spending produces interest charges or a payment you cannot meet.
