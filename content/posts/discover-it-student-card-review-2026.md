I opened my Discover it Student card at 19 and it's still sitting in my wallet three years later, even though it's not my daily driver anymore. That should tell you something. I don't keep accounts around for sentimental reasons. The card still serves a purpose, and if you're looking for your first credit card, it probably deserves a serious look.

This review is based on actually using the card, not just reading the terms page.

## What the Discover it Student Card Actually Offers

The card has no annual fee. The APR range is 18.24% to 27.24% variable, which is standard for student cards. You're not going to get a great rate on a card with no credit history, and that's fine because you shouldn't be carrying a balance anyway. If that's a concern, read [how to pay off credit card debt in college](/posts/how-to-pay-off-credit-card-debt-in-college/) before you apply.

The rewards structure is the interesting part. You earn 5% cash back on rotating quarterly categories up to $1,500 in spending, then 1% after that. Everything outside the categories earns 1% flat. The first year, Discover automatically matches all the cash back you've earned. No cap, no minimum spend requirement to unlock it. If you earn $180 in cash back your first year, you get $360 total. That's a real welcome bonus without the pressure of hitting a spending threshold you might not reach.

Here's a quick comparison against the other cards most students are considering:

| Card | Annual Fee | Rewards | Sign-Up Bonus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover it Student | $0 | 5% rotating / 1% base | First-year cash back match |
| Chase Freedom Rise | $0 | 1.5% flat | $25 statement credit (set up autopay) |
| Capital One Quicksilver Student | $0 | 1.5% flat | None |
| Bank of America Customized Cash Rewards Student | $0 | 3% chosen category / 2% grocery / 1% other | $200 after $1,000 spend in 90 days |
| Deserve EDU Mastercard | $0 | 1% flat | Amazon Prime Student (one year) |

The Discover it Student card doesn't win on simplicity. Chase Freedom Rise and the Quicksilver Student are both easier to use because flat rate cards don't require you to think. But if you're willing to pay attention to the quarterly categories, Discover pays you more.

## The Case For It

The Cashback Match is genuinely good. Most student cards offer a welcome bonus only if you spend $500 or $1,000 within the first three months. For a student with irregular income, that kind of minimum can push you into spending you didn't plan on. Discover's match just requires you to use the card normally for a year. Lower pressure, same reward.

The 5% categories rotate quarterly and have historically included things like gas stations, grocery stores, restaurants, Amazon, and PayPal. Discover announces them in advance, so you can plan. The $1,500 quarterly cap means you're earning 5% on up to $6,000 per year in rotating categories before it drops to 1%. At max, that's $300 in cash back from the rotating categories alone, doubled to $600 in year one.

Discover also doesn't charge a foreign transaction fee, which matters more than people expect once you actually start traveling.

Credit building is where this card quietly does its best work. Discover reports to all three bureaus, includes your FICO score for free on every statement, and has a reputation for being accessible to people with limited or no credit history. If you're starting from scratch, that accessibility matters. [Understanding how credit scores work](/posts/how-credit-scores-work-explained-simply/) will help you get more out of the free score tracking they provide.

## The Honest Downsides

Discover's acceptance has improved a lot, but it's still not Visa or Mastercard. Internationally, it can be hit or miss. Some smaller domestic merchants don't take it either. I've had it declined at exactly the kind of place you'd least expect, a small restaurant, once at a parking garage. It's not a dealbreaker but it's real.

The rotating categories require active management. If you forget to activate the 5% category for the quarter, you earn 1% on everything. I've missed activation before. It's annoying. Some people are good at this kind of thing and some people aren't. Know which one you are before you decide this is the right rewards structure for you.

After your first year, the Cashback Match goes away and the card becomes less competitive against flat rate options. The Bank of America Customized Cash Rewards Student card offers 3% on a category of your choice permanently, which can beat Discover's 1% base rate without any category rotation required. If you're spending heavily in one consistent area like groceries or online shopping, that structure might serve you better long term.

The APR is also worth acknowledging plainly. 18.24% to 27.24% variable is not a loan you want to take out. Carry a balance on this card and the rewards math falls apart fast. The 5% cash back you earned on $300 at a restaurant is wiped out in about five weeks of interest if you're paying the minimum. This card only makes financial sense if you pay it in full every month.

## Who Should Actually Get This Card

If you have no credit history and want a first card that rewards you for using it responsibly, this is probably the best option available right now. The no annual fee structure means you can keep it open indefinitely without paying for the privilege, which matters for your [credit score over time](/posts/how-to-improve-your-credit-score-fast-in-college/).

Students who are good at a little light optimization will get the most out of it. You're not doing anything complicated, you're just activating a quarterly category and using the card at the right places. Five minutes of attention per quarter for meaningfully better rewards.

If you want pure simplicity, the Chase Freedom Rise at 1.5% flat with no category tracking is probably a better fit. There's no shame in that. A card you actually use correctly is worth more than a card with better theoretical returns that you're always half managing.

I applied for the Chase Sapphire Preferred at about 14 months of credit history and got denied. It stung at the time, but that Discover card I'd been ignoring was doing quiet work in the background, building the history I needed to eventually get approved when I reapplied. Don't underestimate what a clean, no-drama student card does for your file over time.

My Discover it Student card is still open. I use it occasionally to keep it active. The credit history it represents, years old with no missed payments, is worth more to me now than any single card's rewards structure. [Knowing what credit score you need for a student credit card](/posts/what-credit-score-do-you-need-for-a-student-credit-card/) when you apply is step one. Knowing what to do with the card once you have it is everything else.

If you're on the fence, the no annual fee removes the main reason to hesitate. Open it, pay it off every month, activate the quarterly categories, and let it work. Year one with the match can realistically get you $200 to $350 back depending on your spending, and that's not nothing on a student budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What credit score do you need to get approved for the Discover it Student card?**
Discover markets this card to people with limited or no credit history, so there's no strict minimum. Many students get approved with scores below 650 or with no credit file at all.

**Q: Does the Cashback Match apply to all cash back earned in year one?**
Yes. Discover matches every dollar of cash back you earn in your first 12 months, including the 5% category rewards and the 1% base rate, with no cap on the match amount.

**Q: Can I use the Discover it Student card internationally?**
You can, and there's no foreign transaction fee. Acceptance varies by country and merchant though, so it's smart to travel with a Visa or Mastercard as a backup.

**Q: What happens to the card after I graduate?**
Discover typically upgrades student cards to a standard Discover it Cash Back card after graduation, which keeps the same core rewards structure. Your account history carries over and your credit line may increase.

**Q: Is it worth keeping the Discover it Student card open after getting a better card?**
Almost always yes. Closing a card reduces your total available credit and shortens your average account age, both of which can hurt your credit score. There's no annual fee to justify closing it, so keeping it open with occasional small purchases is usually the right call.
