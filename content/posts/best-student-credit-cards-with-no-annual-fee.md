A $0 annual fee can be a useful starting filter for a first card. It does not mean the account has no costs: interest, late fees, and transaction fees may still apply.

## Begin with a current shortlist

Use the provider links in [our student first-card comparison](/posts/best-credit-card-for-college-students-with-no-credit-history/) to check the offer available to you. The comparison covers selected options rather than ranking every card in the market.

Capital One's current student page includes student products with no annual fee. Verify the fee and terms on the actual application page before applying. Do not assume an older product name in a review is still available.

## Compare what matters beyond the annual fee

Check whether you meet the issuer's requirements, what purchase APR applies, which fees can arise, and whether the reward rules fit spending you already make. Read any deposit requirements separately from fees.

A bonus is secondary. If meeting a bonus requires buying things outside your budget, the bonus is not a good reason to apply.

## Keep the first account manageable

One affordable account can be easier to monitor than several cards opened for small differences in rewards. Set reminders, inspect statements, and keep money available for payments. Approval and score changes are never guaranteed by the absence of an annual fee.
