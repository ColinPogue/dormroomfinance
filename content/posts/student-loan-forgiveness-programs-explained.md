A claim that loans will be forgiven needs a specific program, eligibility rules, and a current official source. Do not treat a social post or an old article as confirmation that your own balance will be canceled.

## Identify the relevant loan and program

Start with your official federal loan records and current Federal Student Aid information. Public-service programs, repayment-related forgiveness, and discharge provisions can have different requirements. A private loan is governed by a different agreement.

This guide does not promise current enrollment availability, a particular forgiveness timeline, or eligibility for a pending policy proposal.

## Confirm the requirements in writing

Check eligible loan types, qualifying employment or payments where applicable, documentation, deadlines, and the process for submitting a request. Keep records and official responses.

An estimate, a submitted form, or work for a particular employer does not by itself establish approval. Verify how your specific circumstances are recorded.

## Keep payment and tax questions separate

Ask the servicer what payments are required while any request is pending. Do not stop paying based on an unconfirmed assumption. Check current federal and state tax treatment separately before relying on a forgiven balance in a long-term plan.

Avoid paying an unsolicited service for access to a government benefit without checking official channels first. Never share your account password with someone promising guaranteed forgiveness.
