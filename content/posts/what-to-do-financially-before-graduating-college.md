Graduation creates several financial transitions at once. A written checklist is more useful than trying to optimize every decision immediately.

## Map the next three months

Write down reliable take-home income, the first expected payday, housing deposits, moving costs, and essential bills. Keep a cash buffer for gaps between school ending and employment income starting.

A monthly average can hide a problem if rent is due before the first paycheck. Use actual payment dates alongside the [student budget calculator](/tools/student-budget/).

## Gather account and employment information

Keep your offer letter, pay schedule, benefit enrollment deadlines, and official plan documents together. Check when coverage begins and which choices require action. Employer retirement benefits, matching rules, and vesting are plan-specific.

Update contact details with account providers. Use strong, unique passwords and enable available multifactor authentication. Never send sensitive documents through a public comment or an unsolicited message.

## Know the loans you actually have

Identify federal versus private loans, servicers, balances, rates, and the date payments are expected. Use official account records rather than assuming every loan has the same grace period or repayment options.

Do not refinance a federal loan into a private product just because an advertised rate looks lower. That transaction can remove federal protections. Get the current terms and compare the tradeoffs.

## Choose a few sustainable defaults

Plan required payments, set alerts, and review your first complete paycheck before committing to large automatic transfers. Check whether the bank account or student discount you use changes after graduation.
