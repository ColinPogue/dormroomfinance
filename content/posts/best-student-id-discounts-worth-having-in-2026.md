Your student ID is probably the most underused financial asset you have right now. I'm not talking about 10% off at the campus bookstore. I mean real discounts on software, streaming, travel, and services you're already paying full price for without realizing it.

Some of these I've been using since freshman year. Others I only found out about during my internship in New York when I started actually tracking where my money was going. Either way, the math is usually embarrassing once you see it.

## Streaming and Software: The Obvious Ones You Might Still Be Skipping

### Spotify Premium Student

Spotify's student plan is $5.99 a month. The standard individual plan is $10.99. That's $60 a year you're leaving on the table if you haven't switched, and the Spotify student plan currently bundles Hulu's ad-supported tier at no extra cost. So you're getting two subscriptions for less than what most people pay for one. I've had this since sophomore year and it auto-renews every 12 months after a quick re-verification through SheerID. Takes about two minutes.

If you want a full breakdown of how to stack streaming deals as a student, I wrote more about it [here](/posts/how-to-get-apple-music-and-spotify-free-or-cheap-in-college/).

### Apple Music Student

If you're already in the Apple ecosystem, Apple Music's student tier runs $5.99 a month too. Same library, same quality, just verified through UNiDAYS or your school email. I personally use Spotify because of the Hulu bundle, but if you're an Apple loyalist and already have Hulu separately, this probably makes more sense for you.

### Amazon Prime Student

Six months free, then $7.49 a month versus the standard $14.99. You get the same free two-day shipping, Prime Video, and Prime Gaming. The six-month trial alone is worth more than $89 in standard Prime fees. Verify with your .edu email, use the trial aggressively, then decide if $7.49 a month is worth keeping. For me it usually is, mostly because of the shipping during the school year when I'm ordering everything online.

### Microsoft 365 and Adobe Creative Cloud

Check your university's software portal before you pay for either of these. My school gives students full Microsoft 365 for free through a university licensing deal, which includes Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and 1TB of OneDrive storage. A lot of schools do this and students just don't know about it because nobody tells you explicitly.

Adobe is a different story. They don't do free, but the student and teacher plan brings Creative Cloud down to around $19.99 a month from $54.99. If you use Photoshop, Illustrator, or Premiere Pro at all for coursework or side projects, that discount is significant. Just know that pricing can shift slightly, so verify the current rate at adobe.com before you subscribe.

## Travel Discounts That Actually Move the Needle

### Amtrak Student Advantage

This one is completely free to join. Amtrak's Student Advantage program gives you 10% off rail fares just for enrolling with your student email. Not massive, but if you're doing a New York to Boston or DC trip a few times a semester, it adds up. A standard Acela ticket can run $150 to $200 easily. Ten percent off that is real money, especially when the program costs you nothing.

### StudentUniverse for Flights

StudentUniverse isn't a flat percentage discount. It's a booking platform that negotiates student fares directly with airlines. The savings vary a lot depending on route and timing, but I've seen international fares come in $80 to $150 cheaper than what Google Flights was showing for the same trip. You verify your student status once and then search and book through their platform. It's worth checking alongside Google Flights any time you're booking a flight, especially for anything transatlantic.

### Museums, Theater, and Local Entertainment

This category doesn't get enough attention. Most major museums in the US are either free or heavily discounted for students. In New York, the Met technically has a suggested admission of $30 but it's genuinely pay-what-you-wish for students. MoMA offers free admission to full-time students on certain evenings. Broadway theaters often have student rush programs where you can get same-day tickets for $30 to $40 for shows that are otherwise $120 and up. You have to show up in person or check the theater's official site early, but it works.

I used to think these were niche or hard to access. They're not. You just have to look.

## The Discounts People Don't Think to Check

### UNiDAYS and Student Beans

These are aggregator platforms that verify your student status once and then unlock discounts across hundreds of brands. UNiDAYS has deals with Nike (10% off), ASOS (10% off), and a rotating list of tech and lifestyle brands. Student Beans covers some different brands and is worth having both accounts set up since they don't overlap completely.

Neither one requires any payment. You verify with your .edu email or through your institution's portal. It takes maybe five minutes total and then you have access for as long as you're enrolled.

### Apple and Dell Hardware

If you're buying a laptop or tablet while you're a student, do not pay retail. Apple's education store currently knocks $100 off MacBook Air and MacBook Pro models and throws in a $150 App Store gift card through their back-to-school promotion, which usually runs through the summer and into fall. Dell's student store discounts vary but typically run 10% to 15% on select configurations.

I bought my current laptop before I knew this was a thing. That was a frustrating mistake to realize afterward. Check the education store first, always.

### GitHub Student Developer Pack

Free. Completely free. GitHub's student pack bundles together over 100 tools that developers and finance people actually use, including free credits on DigitalOcean, free access to certain JetBrains IDEs, and a free domain through Namecheap for one year. If you're doing anything with code, data, or building side projects, this is an automatic yes. You verify with your school email or GitHub's partner verification system.

## How to Actually Use These Without Forgetting About Them

The mistake most people make is claiming a discount, forgetting about it, and then accidentally paying full price when the subscription renews under a different plan. Set a calendar reminder for 30 days before your expected graduation or student status expiration. That gives you time to downgrade, cancel, or find alternatives before you get charged at the adult rate.

Also, if you're living off campus and managing real monthly expenses the way I was during my first summer in New York, these discounts aren't trivial. I was taking home $3,800 a month and paying $2,100 in rent. Every $10 or $15 a month I wasn't wasting on full-price software was money I could actually work with. If you're navigating a similar situation, [this piece on saving money as an intern in a big city](/posts/how-to-save-money-living-in-a-big-city-as-an-intern/) covers a lot of the same logic applied more broadly.

The other thing worth doing is going through your current subscriptions and actually checking whether you're on the student tier or the standard tier. I audited mine junior year and found I had accidentally rolled off the student Spotify plan after a failed verification. I had been paying $10.99 for two months without noticing. That's not a huge amount but it's the kind of passive drain that compounds when you have six subscriptions doing it at once.

Your student status is temporary. The discounts that come with it have an expiration date. Use them while you have them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a physical student ID to claim most of these discounts?**
Most platforms verify digitally through your .edu email address or through services like SheerID, UNiDAYS, or Student Beans. A physical ID is rarely required for online verification, though some in-person discounts like museum admissions or theater rush tickets will ask to see it.

**Q: What happens to my student discounts when I graduate?**
Most subscriptions will automatically roll to the standard pricing tier at the next renewal after your student status expires. Some platforms give you a grace period and others cut access immediately, so it's worth knowing the policy for each service you use before graduation hits.

**Q: Is Amazon Prime Student worth keeping after the free trial?**
At $7.49 a month it depends entirely on how much you actually use it. If you're ordering things regularly and watching Prime Video, probably yes. If you only used it for the trial period deals, cancel before the trial ends and revisit it later. There's no penalty for canceling.

**Q: Can I use multiple student discount aggregators at the same time?**
Yes, and you should. UNiDAYS and Student Beans cover different brand partnerships, so having both accounts set up costs you nothing and means you're more likely to find a deal regardless of where you're shopping.

**Q: Are student hardware discounts on Apple products combinable with other sales?**
Generally no. Apple's education pricing is its own track and doesn't stack with other promotional discounts. That said, the back-to-school gift card promotion often runs simultaneously with education pricing, so you can effectively get both benefits at once during that window.
