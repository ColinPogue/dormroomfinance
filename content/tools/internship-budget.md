An internship paycheck may feel like extra money until the rent deposit, commute, and move back to campus arrive. This worksheet puts those costs next to your expected income before you commit.

Open the CSV in Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets. It includes a worked example, a blank amount column, and notes. No email or signup required. It contains no spreadsheet formulas; calculate the totals using the steps below or the [budget calculator](/tools/student-budget/).

## Fill it in before signing a lease

1. Enter the internship length and actual take-home pay per week. Gross salary is not the amount available to spend.
2. Add rent, utilities, food, transport, and other weekly costs for the full placement.
3. Separate a refundable housing deposit from nonrefundable moving costs. A deposit still ties up cash even when you expect it back.
4. Add a reserve for the weeks after the internship, before your next reliable source of income.
5. Subtract total costs and reserves from total take-home pay plus cash you are willing to use. If the result is negative, change the housing or transport plan before committing.

## Example: a ten-week placement

These are illustrative amounts, not typical wages or living costs. At $700 take-home per week, ten weeks produces $7,000. If housing costs $3,000, other living expenses total $2,000, moving costs $400, and the post-internship reserve $1,000, the remaining amount is $600 **before** any refundable deposit or additional costs.

A $1,000 deposit would require additional cash up front even if you expect to get it back later. Timing matters as much as the overall total.
